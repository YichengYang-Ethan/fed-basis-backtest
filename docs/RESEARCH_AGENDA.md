# Research agenda: FOMC futures–prediction-market relative value

Prepared 24 September 2026 from the existing local research and the 20 September depth replay. Public research agenda. These are proposed experiments; no performance improvement is asserted until it survives the relevant test.

The useful starting point is a testable economic proposition: calendar-matched ZQ futures and decision-contingent digital contracts can disagree about the same policy event. A calendar spread removes a common interest-rate level under the specified assumptions; it does not remove every future rate-path or financing exposure. The observed price differences and audited historical cash flows justify further investigation. Whether they represent executable relative value, compensation for omitted risks, or an inventory/market-making opportunity remains an empirical question. The nine-trade account evidence is **CME–Polymarket**. Kalshi is part of the contract framework and available data, but has not produced an equivalent nine-trade execution-and-capital result here.

The agenda below is ordered by economic importance and what existing data can answer. It proposes mechanisms and falsifiable comparisons rather than performance improvements already established.

1. **Exit horizon versus the loss of spread-margin relief.** In the prior nine-trade funding analysis, the remaining far-month stage determines every standalone support requirement. Holding to final cash settlement therefore trades payment certainty for a longer period of outright-rate exposure and funding. Compare the unchanged terminal-hold baseline with a small, predetermined set of operational exits: before near-leg expiry, and the first specified executable window after the announcement. BACK can expire its near leg before the announcement, so those are economically different hypotheses. Measure net dollars, peak cash need, capital-days, and unfinished-leg losses. Existing records support calendar/cash decomposition; exit books, actual FCM margin schedules, and transfer/release times remain necessary. Reject a capital-efficiency gain if exit costs or failure losses consume the saving. Freeze exits before evaluating new events; never choose each event’s best exit retrospectively.

2. **Choose the contract for capital and execution, not just the largest displayed basis.** FRONT/BACK spans, expiry order, quoted depth, and native-versus-synthetic execution change the dollars earned and capital required for the same policy sensitivity. At common decision timestamps, compare all calendar-eligible structures at a fixed policy exposure, including costs, cash phases, and route-specific capacity. Existing calendars, definitions, and quotes can build the eligibility map; simultaneous depth for every candidate and account-specific spread credits are incomplete. Reject apparent superiority driven by stale quotes, unequal exposure, other meetings’ cash-flow effects, or duplicated implied liquidity. Freeze a deployable selection objective before the next sample; historical profit must not select the winning contract.

3. **YES/NO simplification with an explicit state-risk budget.** A single NO position can reduce legs and sometimes change funding or executable cost. It does not make omitted outcomes disappear: NO(HOLD) also pays on cuts and larger hikes. Compare actual YES and NO books and a full ladder using a dollar payoff matrix over every contractual state, including intervals within open-ended buckets. Minimize executable cost subject to a specified tail-loss and funding limit, rather than assuming two-state equivalence globally. Existing rules permit preliminary payoff algebra; historical rule versions and alternative-token depth are needed for trade comparisons. Reject a hedge if bucket-internal moves defeat the claimed bound or protection costs exhaust the advantage. Specify the state set and risk limits beforehand; do not set them from the nine observed winners.

4. **Separate forward carry and residual risk from the decision-price discrepancy.** The realized monthly EFFR residual is computable after settlement, but is not known at entry. Likewise, a futures-implied mean is not automatically a physical probability; converting it into a digital probability requires a state-support model and compatible pricing assumptions. Decompose conditional quote advantage, realized EFFR residual, integer hedge error, and fees. Then test a simple, economically specified residual model using only previously released EFFR, administered-rate spreads, and calendar information; include unscheduled policy moves and off-grid decisions as scenarios. Existing FRED/settlement histories support exploratory work; first-release vintages, exact settlement records, and payout rules are needed for stronger certification. Reject a residual adjustment if its forward errors or unhedged states outweigh its benefit. Use chronological training and a zero-adjustment benchmark; never credit average historical residual gains as free alpha.

5. **Joint execution and nonlinear position sizing.** Fixed cents-per-share costs cannot capture depth, partial fills, or replenishment. The new replay already walks five historical ask books and maximizes contemporaneous two-state net payoff over feasible integer sizes; it has not optimized real execution. Test a fixed sequencing, latency, cancellation, and emergency-unwind policy using synchronized books and prospective shadow orders. Record every qualified candidate, including failures and zero-size decisions. Existing five books and nine CME entry days support bounded diagnostics; order acknowledgments/fills, alternative-token books, historical fees, and actual intraday margin calls are the next inputs. Reject scalable execution if missed fills, adverse selection, or stressed cash demands exceed the quoted benefit. Choose deployment sizes and the latency policy before observing future P&L; do not infer capacity from the old filled cases alone.

6. **Use SR1/SR3 as an independent policy-curve check.** A second curve can distinguish a common policy-path discrepancy from a ZQ-specific EFFR basis or stale quote. It also introduces SOFR–EFFR basis risk. First clean the already acquired statistics by outright contract, settlement type, publication/receipt time, preliminary/final status, and duplicates. Respect SR1 averaging and SR3 compounding and reference periods before deriving policy sensitivities. Compare matched-exposure ZQ and SOFR implications on common dates; retain their disagreement as a diagnostic before using SOFR as a signal or hedge. Existing statistics permit this first test without another purchase; intraday SOFR books and richer curve inputs would be needed for execution. Reject incremental value if the apparent signal is explained by basis or fixing-window mismatch. Freeze the mapping and benchmark before viewing strategy returns.

7. **Distinguish persistent mispricing from asynchronous price discovery.** High price-level correlation can coexist with little contemporaneous quote-change correlation. Test whether economically meaningful discrepancies survive both-clock freshness, executable size, and a fixed order-arrival delay. Preserve a coverage ledger of every eligible meeting and failed attempt. Historical quote data support descriptive diagnostics; prospective complete candidate-book capture provides the cleaner next test, separately for Polymarket and Kalshi. Reject the executable hypothesis if the discrepancy is confined to stale observations or disappears before orders can arrive. Freeze a small mechanism-based comparison family, cluster inference by meeting, and reserve untouched chronological events; millions of minute rows do not supply millions of independent trials.

The next stage should settle the mandate: terminal relative value, bounded-risk event trading, or a quoting/hedging signal. It should also define accessible venues, minimum useful dollar capacity, acceptable unmatched exposure, and whether capital is assessed standalone or incrementally against a real portfolio. Those choices determine the appropriate objective before any parameter search.

Crucial unknowns remain historical fee and rule versions, actual venue eligibility, payment and transfer timing, account/FCM margin and intraday cash calls, complete candidate and exit depth, and the joint distribution of tail outcomes, EFFR residuals, and execution failures. A per-outcome <=5% quote filter does not cap aggregate tail probability at 5%, and prices are not established physical probabilities. “No modeled maintenance shortfall” is narrower than “cannot be liquidated.” The latest replay is a fixed-rule cost-and-cash re-estimation, not a new optimal-arbitrage claim.
